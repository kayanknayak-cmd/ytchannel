Status: v1 clip-art visuals retired. Rebuild once archive hosts are reachable.

## Fact check

| On-screen claim | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A Coke cost 5¢ from 1886 to 1959 (73 years) | Verified. Most outlets round to "70 years" | Levy & Young (2004), JMCB 36(4); NPR Planet Money ep. 416 |
| 1899 bottling contract fixed the syrup price | Verified. Syrup sold to bottlers at a fixed price; contract renegotiated in 1921, so "locked in" is true of the 1899 deal, not forever | Levy & Young; NPR |
| Vending machines took one coin, a nickel | Verified | NPR Planet Money; Levy & Young |
| Coke asked the President for a 7½¢ coin | Verified. Woodruff asked Eisenhower in 1953; Treasury declined | Wikipedia "Fixed price of Coca-Cola from 1886 to 1959" (cites Levy & Young) |
| Nickel Coke ended in 1959 | Verified ("the last nickel Coke seems to have been in 1959") | NPR / Levy & Young |

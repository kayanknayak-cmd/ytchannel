# Scripts v2 (02-10): every hook rebuilt for viral odds, all <= ~90 words (45s at Adam's pace ~2.2 w/s incl. pauses)
Hook formula: "You're a scientist, and you're about to [do something wrong/absurd to a person]." Payoff turns on the viewer. Loops into opening.

02 The Wrong Line (Asch 1951; ~75% conformed at least once)
03 The Button (Milgram 1961-63; 26/40 = 65% to max; psychiatrists predicted ~0.1%)
04 The Marshmallow (Mischel; Watts/Duncan/Quan 2018 reanalysis; Kidd 2013 reliability)
05 The Prison (Zimbardo 1971; Le Texier 2018 coaching evidence; contested by Zimbardo)
06 The Smoke (Latane & Darley 1968: alone 75%, two passive confederates 10%)
07 The Gorilla (Simons & Chabris 1999: ~50% missed). VISUAL GAG: set wall color must change mid-video.
08 Free Chocolate (Shampanier, Mazar, Ariely 2007: Lindt 15c/Kiss 1c -> 73% Lindt; 14c/free -> 69% Kiss) https://www-2.rotman.utoronto.ca/facbios/file/ZeroPrice.pdf
09 The Sermon (Darley & Batson 1973: high hurry 10% helped; message not significant)
10 The $20 Auction (Shubik 1971 dollar auction; Bazerman $20 auctions, record $204 per secondary sources, proceeds to charity)

Dropped: Jam study (contested, weak payoff), Mirror study (soft payoff; 9% vs 38% verified, kept as backup).

## Tagged scripts (sent to owner)
02 [playful] You're a scientist, and you're about to make a man lie about something he can see with his own eyes. Three lines. Which one matches? It's obviously C. But the seven people next to him all work for you. And one by one, they say A. [pause] His turn. He squints. He knows it's C. [whispering] He says A. Three out of four people caved at least once. Not because they were blind. Because being the only one who's right feels worse than being wrong. [playful] So, what's the answer? You're a scientist...
03 [softly] You're a scientist, and you're about to find out how far a normal person will go if you just ask nicely. Every wrong answer, the volunteer presses a button. The man next door gets shocked. Harder each time. He starts yelling. The volunteer looks at you. [slowly] Please continue. [whispering] The man's an actor. The shocks are fake. Experts guessed almost nobody would go to the max. [pause] Two out of three did. Nobody forced them. Someone in a lab coat just said please. [playful] So, who's asking you nicely? You're a scientist...
04 [playful] You're a scientist, and you're about to predict a kid's whole future with one marshmallow. Eat it now, or wait fifteen minutes and get two. Then you walk out. Some kids eat it instantly. [chuckling] Some lick it and put it back. Years later, the ones who waited have better test scores. The internet loses it. [pause] Then someone reruns it with way more kids. Account for family money, and the magic mostly disappears. Maybe those kids weren't impatient. Maybe they'd learned that adults don't always come back. [softly] Would you wait? You're a scientist...
05 [playful] You're a scientist, and you're about to create the most famous lesson in psychology. And it's fake. Okay, not fake. Complicated. You build a prison in a basement. Coin flip: guards or prisoners. Six days in, it's chaos, and you shut it down. The lesson: give normal people power, and they turn cruel. [pause] Decades later, the tapes come out. The guards were coached. Told to be tough. Some said they were just acting. [whispering] So who really turned cruel? The guy running it. [slowly] That's you. You're a scientist...
06 [playful] You're a scientist, and you're about to fill a room with smoke and watch someone just sit there. Alone, most students get up and report it within minutes. Now two strangers sit next to them. Your actors. Smoke pours in. The actors shrug and keep writing. [pause] And the student? Coughs. Waves it away. Keeps writing. Alone, three out of four reported it. With two calm strangers, one in ten. [sighing] Everyone's waiting for someone else to panic first. [playful] Would you get up? You're a scientist...
07 [playful] You're a scientist, and you're about to make people miss a gorilla. A whole gorilla. You show them a video. Count the passes by the team in white. Halfway through, someone in a gorilla suit strolls into the middle, beats its chest, [chuckling] and walks off. Afterwards you ask: see anything weird? Half say no. When your brain locks onto one job, it deletes everything else. [whispering] Like the wall behind me. [pause] Did you notice it changed color? You're a scientist...
08 [playful] You're a scientist, and you're about to make people choose worse chocolate. Fancy truffle: fifteen cents. Cheap little chocolate kiss: one cent. Most people pay up for the truffle. Easy. Now drop both by one cent. Truffle: fourteen. Kiss: free. [pause] Same gap. Same chocolate. And now most people grab the cheap one. [chuckling] That's the power of free. It switches your brain off. It's why so many apps are free to download. [whispering] And why you're still watching this. You're a scientist...
09 [playful] You're a scientist, and you're about to make future priests walk past a man in need. You ask seminary students to give a talk. Some get the Good Samaritan, the story about stopping to help a stranger. On the way, they pass a man slumped in a doorway. Your actor. Who stops? The speech barely mattered. [pause] One thing did. You told some of them: you're late, hurry. Rushed, only one in ten stopped. [chuckling] Some stepped right over him, on their way to talk about helping people. [playful] Are you in a hurry right now? You're a scientist...
10 [playful] You're a scientist, and you're about to sell a twenty dollar bill for way more than twenty dollars. Simple auction. Highest bid wins the twenty. One catch: second place pays too, and gets nothing. Bids start at a dollar. Easy money. Then it hits nineteen. Twenty. [pause] Now the guy in second can lose twenty, or bid twenty one... for a twenty. [chuckling] He bids. One professor ran this for years. The record? Two hundred and four dollars. [whispering] For a twenty. Ever stayed in something just because you'd already put so much in? You're a scientist...

# 12. Why plane windows are round

Niche: science

## Script
> Airplane windows used to be square. Then in 1954, two jets broke apart in midair, three months apart. The de Havilland Comet, the world's first jet airliner. Investigators found metal fatigue. Every flight the cabin gets pressurized, and tiny cracks grow at sharp corners, where stress piles up. On one plane it started at a squared-off cutout in the roof. Round corners spread that stress out. So look at the window next to you. Notice the corners? Because ||

## Notes
Loop: "Notice the corners? Because / airplane windows used to be square."
Visuals: Comet photos (Wikimedia/PD UK gov), crack drawn with marker from a corner, stress arrows.
Sources: [FAA Lessons Learned](https://www.faa.gov/lessons_learned/transport_airplane/accidents/G-ALYV); [Admiral Cloudberg](https://admiralcloudberg.medium.com/neither-money-nor-manpower-the-story-of-the-de-havilland-comet-and-the-crash-of-boac-flight-781-36db2a3435ce); [Wikipedia, SAA 201](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Airways_Flight_201).

# 07. Money at the bottom of the sea

Niche: econ

## Script
> One family was rich because of a stone at the bottom of the ocean. On the island of Yap, money was giant carved stones. Some taller than a person. Way too heavy to move, so mostly, they didn't. Everyone just remembered who owned which one. And one stone sank on the trip home, but everybody agreed it was down there, so it still counted. Which, honestly, is kind of how your bank account works. And it's why ||

`||` marks the loop point: the last line runs straight into the first.

## Notes
Loop: "And it's why / one family was rich because of a stone at the bottom of the ocean."
Visuals: rai stone photos (Furness 1910, PD), ocean-blue paper, ledger typewriter.
Sources: [NPR Planet Money](https://www.npr.org/sections/money/2011/02/15/131934618/the-island-of-stone-money); [Milton Friedman, "The Island of Stone Money" (1991)](https://miltonfriedman.hoover.org/objects/56723/the-island-of-stone-money); [Wikipedia, Rai stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_stones). Sunken-stone story is from anthropologist William Furness's 1910 account.
